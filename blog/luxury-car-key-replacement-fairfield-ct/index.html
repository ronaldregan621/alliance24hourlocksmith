The Scam You Need to Know About Before You Search for a Locksmith

If you Google "locksmith near me" after losing your BMW key in Greenwich, the first results may be paid ads quoting $35 to $75 for a car key service. That price is a bait and switch. It is not a real price.

The low ad price is always the setup, not the price. A scam operation buys a Google ad using a local Connecticut phone number. When you call, you reach a call center that quotes an impossibly low price and sends a contractor in an unmarked vehicle. Once the job is done, the price jumps to $400 to $700. It is late, you are alone in a parking lot, your car is already open, and they demand cash or Zelle only.

Real accounts describe the same pattern: the low phone quote, the unmarked car, the dramatically higher number once the work is done. One account described a $600 charge for a simple unlock. The receipt has no business name, no address, no license number. Any locksmith who refuses to quote over the phone, shows up unmarked, or demands Zelle is running this scam.

What Moves the Final Price Up or Down on Luxury Car Key Replacement in Fairfield CT

Within the ranges above, a few variables determine where your final price lands.

  • Model year. Vehicles from 2020 onward often have additional security layers that add programming time. Pre-2015 models are generally faster.
  • Key type. A basic transponder key costs less than a full proximity key with passive entry. Smart keys with comfort access are at the higher end.
  • Working keys remaining. One working key means faster programming. Zero working keys means we work from the VIN and ownership documents, adding $50 to $100 and some time.
  • Time of day. Standard hours run roughly 8am to 8pm. After hours calls carry a premium we disclose before dispatch.
